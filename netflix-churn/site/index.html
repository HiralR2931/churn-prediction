Churn falls off cliffs, not slopes

Two habits carry most of the signal: how much people watch, and how long ago they last logged in. Neither changes smoothly. Churn drops in steps at 5 and 20 watch hours, and jumps once the last login is more than 30 days old.

      Method and caveats

      The data is synthetic. It is a public Kaggle dataset, not Netflix's records. Its sharp thresholds, a derived column and features with no effect at all show it was generated from rules. The findings describe this dataset; they say nothing about real Netflix customers.

      Thresholds. The cut-points (5 and 20 watch hours, 30 days) are where churn jumps in the data and where a single split separates churners best. The points were found by searching small integers for the highest accuracy; no other small weights for these six conditions do better. The cut-points were chosen on the full dataset, so the held-out scorecard accuracy is very slightly optimistic (the fixed card scores 95.6% on all rows).

      Evaluation. Repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation, 3 repeats, the same folds for every model. Feature tests use chi-square for categories and point-biserial correlation for numbers.
